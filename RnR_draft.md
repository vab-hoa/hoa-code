## Introduction: The Governing Framework

The Villas at the Boulders operates under a layered set of governing authorities. Understanding how they fit together helps every homeowner know where to look for the rules that apply to them.

At the top is Colorado state law, principally the Colorado Common Ownership Interest Act (CCIOA, CRS § 38-33.3), which establishes the legal framework for all common interest communities in Colorado. Below that are the Association's foundational documents: the **Declaration of Covenants, Conditions, and Restrictions**, recorded in Broomfield County on July 1, 2002, and the **Bylaws of the Association**. These documents define the rights and obligations of every owner and establish the authority of the Board.

The Covenants and Bylaws authorize the Board to act in several ways. First, the Board adopts **Policies** — formal governance documents addressing specific topics such as covenant enforcement, collection of unpaid assessments, and alternative dispute resolution. The Association currently has fifteen adopted policies, all available on the Association website. Second, the Board, acting on recommendations from the Architectural Review Committee, adopts **ARC Design Guidelines**, which govern the exterior appearance of the community and any modifications owners wish to make. Third, under Article II, Section 2.52 of the Declaration, the Board has authority to adopt additional rules governing the use and enjoyment of the property. **This document records those rules.**

In any conflict between these layers, the higher authority governs: state law supersedes everything; the Covenants and Bylaws supersede policies, guidelines, and these rules. These rules are intended to be consistent with, and to clarify the application of, the Covenants. Where a section of the Covenants is referenced, owners are encouraged to read the original text.

Contact information for the Association manager and Board, and links to all governing documents, are available on the Association website at villasboulders.org.

## 1. Pets
*(Declaration §4.8, Animals and Pets)*

Each villa may house a maximum of two pets: two dogs, two cats, or one of each. No other animals may be kept, including livestock, fowl, exotic animals, wildlife, endangered or poisonous species, or beehives. Dog runs and doghouses are not permitted on Association property. Breeding animals for commercial purposes is prohibited.

The Association prohibits "high-risk" dog breeds, including but not limited to: American Pit Bull Terrier, American Staffordshire Terrier, Staffordshire Bull Terrier, American Bulldog, Dogo Argentino, Canary Dog (Presa Canario), Presa Mallorquin, Tosa Inu, Cane Corso, and Fila Brasileiro, as well as any mixed-breed or hybrid wolf dog with characteristics of these breeds. The Board may also order the removal of any animal that, in its reasonable judgment, poses a current or future health or safety risk to the community.

Dogs must wear a current city license tag and current immunization tag at all times when outside. All animals must be on a leash when outside a residence. Pets must not be left on patios or upper decks for extended periods, and never when the owner is away from the villa. Pets are not permitted to urinate or defecate on upper decks or patios. Owners must immediately collect and properly dispose of all pet waste — in pet waste receptacles or in their own covered trash container. Any damage to Association property or private property caused by a pet is the owner's responsibility.

Owners observing a violation should contact Broomfield Animal Control and may send a written complaint with the date, location, animal description, and violating owner's address to the Managing Agent. Complaints cannot be acted on without the address of the violator.

## 2. Parking
*(Declaration §4.9(b), Parking and Prohibited Vehicles)*

Owners' vehicles must ordinarily be parked in the owner's garage or on the owner's driveway. Vehicles parked on community streets between midnight and 6:00 am are subject to fines or towing at the owner's expense. Vehicles must not encroach on curbs or protrude over lawns or stone areas.

When temporary overflow parking on a street is necessary, the owner must notify the Board in advance and obtain approval for each night the vehicle will be parked. The request must include the vehicle's make, color, and license plate number. This is intended for genuine short-term needs, not routine use of street parking.

Stored vehicles that are inoperable or lack current operating licenses must be kept in garages. Commercial vehicles exceeding a 3/4-ton rating may not be parked anywhere in the community except for vehicles making pickups or deliveries or performing community maintenance. Pickup trucks used for non-commercial purposes by an owner or permittee are permitted. All commercial vehicles displaying advertising that are used for commercial purposes must be stored in a garage, not on driveways or streets. Recreational vehicles — including trailers, motor homes, boats, and watercraft — must be stored in garages.

The community speed limit is 25 mph unless otherwise posted. Excessive vehicle noise is prohibited.

## 3. Patios and Decks
*(Declaration §2.52)*

Patios and upper decks are limited common elements: the Association owns and maintains them, but each owner has exclusive use of the patio and upper deck assigned to their villa. This section governs what owners may do with those spaces.

Seasonal patio furniture, planters with attractive fresh or artificial flowers, and grills are permitted on patios and upper decks. Grills may also be stored there. Grills may not be stored or operated on front porches, driveways, grass areas, or stone areas around villa foundations. Grills must be attended at all times when in use; owners are encouraged to keep a fire extinguisher readily available. No outdoor carpet may be permanently glued or affixed to patios or upper decks. No item other than seasonal patio furniture and grills may be permanently stored on patios or upper decks — firewood storage on patios, upper decks, or anywhere else on the villa exterior is not permitted, as it creates conditions favorable to insect infestation.

The vinyl privacy fences between adjoining patios carry a manufacturer's limited lifetime warranty that is voided by penetrating the fence for any reason or by placing a hot grill directly against it. No modifications or attachments of any kind are permitted to these fences. Owners causing damage to the privacy fences are liable for the cost of repair or replacement.

Owners are responsible for keeping patios and upper decks clean and free of accumulated leaves, debris, and dirt, as these materials impede drainage and drying. Non-permanent decorative objects in good condition are welcome on patios, porches, sidewalks, and rock areas, provided they do not impede access. The Association may require removal of items deemed unsightly or unkempt under Declaration §4.5.

## 4. Signage
*(CRS §38-33.3-106.5; Declaration §4.7)*

Colorado law protects an owner's right to display political and advocacy signs within the windows of their unit. No Association rule restricts that right.

Signs placed in the common area in front of a unit are subject to the following:

- **Real estate signs:** Temporary "for sale" or "for rent" signs of professional quality are permitted, placed in the rock area in front of the villa. "Open house" signs may be placed on general common elements for one day at a time and must be removed at the end of that day.
- **Security signs:** Security alarm company signs must be placed close to the villa, in rock areas.
- **Non-commercial signs:** Signs placed in the rock area in front of the villa must not exceed 24" × 32". No more than four signs may be placed in the common area at any time.
- **Prohibited:** Commercial signs are prohibited by the Covenants. House numbers other than those installed by the Association are prohibited.

## 5. Trees
*(Declaration §4.7(c), Landscaping)*

Trees are HOA property and a significant community investment. Colorado's climate is hard on trees, and their care requires consistent protection. No owner may plant, remove, or alter any tree or landscaping.

Nothing may be hung from or attached to trees — including bird feeders, decorations, flower baskets, light chains, or any other item — except for temporary holiday lighting. Holiday lighting may be hung from November 1 through January 31 and must be removed by January 31. It may not be installed in any way that damages the tree. Outside of that holiday window, trees must be left undisturbed.

Any owner who damages a tree may be fined and held responsible for the cost of replacement.

## 6. Trash and Waste
*(Declaration §2.52; Declaration §4.5)*

Trash is collected weekly from driveways at the street. Trash containers must be kept inside garages at all times except when placed for pickup. Containers may be placed curbside after 5:00 pm the evening before collection and must be returned to the garage by 9:00 pm on the day of collection. Recycling service is available — contact the Managing Agent for details.

For owners who will be away during a collection window, the options are heavy-duty plastic bags placed at the curb on collection day or arranging for a neighbor's assistance.

Disposal of household hazardous waste in trash containers is prohibited by law. Hazardous materials include vehicle batteries, motor oil, paint, tires, propane tanks, garden chemicals, and gasoline — the trash removal company cannot accept these items. They may be dropped off at the City of Broomfield's Household Hazardous Waste facility; see the City's website for current hours and locations. If the Association incurs costs related to a resident's improper hazardous waste disposal, the owner will be billed for those costs plus a $50.00 fine at the Board's discretion.

## 7. Rental Rules
*(Declaration §4.2, Leasing of Lots; §4.3, Subdivision of Lot; Time-Sharing)*

Owners who lease their villa are responsible for ensuring that their tenants comply with all Association governing documents — the Covenants, Bylaws, policies, ARC Guidelines, and these rules. Subletting by tenants is not permitted.

Before or within ten days of a tenant moving in, the owner must provide the Managing Agent with a completed tenant agreement in which the lessee(s) acknowledge and agree to all Association rules and conditions. The current form is available from the Managing Agent. Owners remain liable for violations by their tenants.

Short-term rentals through platforms such as Airbnb, VRBO, or similar services are prohibited under Declaration §4.3, which bars any time-sharing, fraction-sharing, or similar program in which the right to exclusive occupancy of a Lot rotates among users on a short-term or scheduled basis.

## 8. Exterior Conditions and Vehicle Maintenance
*(Declaration §4.5, Unsightly or Unkempt Conditions; §4.9, Parking and Prohibited Vehicles)*

The Covenants require that all portions of a Lot outside of enclosed structures be kept in a clean and tidy condition at all times. Nothing shall be stored, maintained, or kept outside of enclosed structures in a condition that the Board determines to be unclean, unhealthy, untidy, or obnoxious to the senses. Any equipment or items placed on the exterior of a Lot must be kept in a neat, clean, and attractive condition and shall be removed promptly upon Board request if they have become rusty, dilapidated, or fallen into disrepair.

Note that driveways are limited common elements — they are Association property allocated for each owner's exclusive use, not private property. The same standards that apply to patios, lawns, and other exterior areas apply to driveways. Trash containers must be kept in garages except when placed at the curb for pickup (see Section 6, Trash). Equipment, vehicles, and personal property left on driveways are subject to the same clean and tidy standard as any other exterior area.

The Covenants also specifically address tarpaulin-covered vehicles. A vehicle covered with a tarpaulin and remaining so covered for seven consecutive days is defined by the Covenants as a "stored" vehicle (§4.9). Stored vehicles are required to be kept in enclosed garages. Keeping a tarped vehicle in a driveway beyond that threshold is a covenant violation, regardless of whether it is being worked on.

Vehicle and mechanical work on the exterior of a Lot is addressed separately. The assembly and disassembly of motor vehicles and other mechanical devices in driveways or other outdoor areas — activity that tends to create disorderly, unsightly, or unkempt conditions — is prohibited unless it is begun and completed within 12 hours and is not done on a regular or frequent basis (§4.5). Ongoing or recurring mechanical work outdoors is a violation whether or not the equipment is covered between sessions.

Owners should be aware that Colorado law (CCIOA, CRS §38-33.3) provides homeowners with procedural protections in enforcement matters, including notice and an opportunity to be heard before fines are imposed. The Association is committed to enforcing these standards fairly and consistently through the process set out in the Covenant Enforcement Policy.

## 9. Miscellaneous Rules
*(Declaration §2.52)*

The following rules do not require extended treatment but carry the same authority as all rules in this document.

**Noise.** Excessive noise is prohibited at all times (Declaration §4.6). Quiet hours are 11:00 pm to 7:00 am. Noise that will not be tolerated at any hour includes amplified music, parties, and live performances whether from residences, garages, vehicles, or common areas.

**Garage Sales.** Individual garage sales are prohibited. The Board may designate a date for a community-wide garage sale open to all owners. Moving sales and similar one-time events require prior Board approval.

**Firewood.** Storage of firewood is not permitted anywhere on the exterior of a villa, including patios and upper decks.

**Broken Pipes.** During freezing weather, owners should disconnect garden hoses from exterior hose bibs and should not turn off heat entirely when away from the villa.

**Volunteer Work.** Volunteer work by community members is encouraged. Volunteers may not be compensated for their time, as compensation creates insurance and liability complications for the Association.
