The HOA’s Data
The HOA has a bunch of documents, spreadsheets, policy and governing document stuff, newsletters, financial reports, etc. We’ve attempted different ways of managing this over our ~25 years. In the years before 2020 we had some data on a shared google drive of a google account (villas.boulders.board@gmail.com). After 2020, we moved a lot of our data to a new portal system offered by the management company (then advancehoa), and a lot of effort was expended trying to organize it and populate it. Then we switched management companies in 2024, and a great deal of our data was lost.


Beginning in 2025 we obtained a “google workspace”. This is a construct offered by google organized around an internet domain name (for us villasboulders.org), which offers a lot of related services: mail, forms, websites, documents, spreadsheets, surveys, contacts. We began to move our data here, and to set up the website, the google drive (with our files), forms (for ARC, LBC, etc), mailing lists (e.g. board@villasboulders.org) and other features. 


One aspect of this workspace is  a shared google drive capability. A central account (admin@villasboulders.org) sets up a drive which is shared with a group of people. Everyone in the group has access to the drive. For us one drive is shared with board@villasboulders.org, and the other is shared with all residents, using a couple of lists (owners@villasboulders.org & non-owner-occupants@villasboulders.org). 
The Shared Drives
The HOA uses three Google shared drives, visible under Shared drives in your Google Drive sidebar:


HOA Board Documents — your primary workspace. Contains everything the board works with: meeting materials, budgets, financials, insurance, work orders, contracts, owner issues, policies, and more. Only board members can see this.


HOA Documents — content that homeowners can also see. Budgets, financials, governing documents, policies, newsletters, meeting minutes, maps, forms, and reserve studies. You rarely need to go here directly — see the shortcuts below.


HOA Website — this houses the actual code of the website, which is a “google sites” app. This directory doesn’t have but one file in it.

Getting There
1. Go to drive.google.com and sign in with the Google account that was given board access.
2. In the left sidebar, click Shared drives. You should see three VaB drives listed.
3. If you don't see Shared drives in the sidebar, look for a small arrow or "More" link to expand it.


On mobile, open the Google Drive app, tap the menu (≡), and tap Shared drives.


If you can't see either drive, your account hasn't been added yet — contact the IT Officer.

The Keystone Cache and the Keystone Scraper
There is stuff on the Keystone Portal that is important to us. This is where Josh keeps track of Work Orders, ARC Requests and LBC Requests, Violations, Delinquencies, and where he posts minutes and financials. This is pretty basic data that we sometimes need. Our contract with Keystone stipulates that they will keep the data, and the Keystone portal is what they give us. But if we move to another management company, we lose access to this data. We saw how that works in 2024. We can’t let that happen again. 


So, we gather the data from the Keystone website, and put it into our own files. We have a big spreadsheet called the Keystone Cache which has sheets for Work Orders, ARC Requests, Violations, etc. And we have code that runs every night and gets any new data from Keystone. At the same time it looks for other new files, like new copies of financial reports, or new minutes.

What Goes Where
The rule: Everything homeowners can see lives in HOA Documents. HOA Board Documents holds only what's private to the board, plus the → shortcuts (see below about this feature) as a convenience window into Homeowner Documents.


Uploading something homeowner-visible (new bylaws, published minutes, approved budget, newsletter): use the → shortcut in Board Documents to navigate to the right folder, then upload there. The file lands in Homeowner Documents — homeowners see it immediately, no second step needed.


Uploading something board-only (draft minutes, owner correspondence, insurance documents, working notes): upload directly to the Board Documents folder. It never touches Homeowner Documents.


When in doubt: if a homeowner could reasonably ask to see it, use the → shortcut and put it in Homeowner Documents. If it's internal board work, it stays in Board Documents.

Uploading and Editing
* To upload a file: navigate to the right folder, then drag and drop, or use the + New button.
* Google Docs, Sheets, and Slides all work normally — changes save automatically.
* Word, Excel, and PDF files can be uploaded and opened for viewing. Google Drive can also convert them to Google format for editing if you choose.
* To create a new document in the drive: navigate to the folder, click + New, choose the file type. The file is stored in the drive from the start, not in your personal Drive.


________________


Searching
The search bar at the top of Drive searches across everything you have access to, including shared drives. To limit a search to just Board Documents or Homeowner Documents, navigate into that drive first, then search.


________________


A Few Things to Know
Files belong to the organization. Files in shared drives are owned by the HOA, not by you personally. If you leave the board, the files stay. This also means you won't see these files under "My Drive" — they live only under Shared drives.


Don't share individual files directly with homeowners. If a homeowner needs something, the right place for it is Homeowner Documents. Sharing individual Board Documents files directly bypasses that separation.


Cross-drive drag and drop doesn't work. You can't drag a file from Board Documents into Homeowner Documents (or vice versa) in the Drive UI. If you need a file in both places, use File → Make a copy and upload the copy to the other drive.


Deleted files go to the drive's Trash, not your personal trash. Only drive members can restore them. If you accidentally delete something, let the IT Officer know quickly — items stay in Trash for 30 days before permanent deletion.


________________


Access and Permissions
Board members are added as Content managers, which means you can upload, edit, move, and delete files but cannot change drive membership or settings. If you need to add someone or change permissions, contact the IT Officer.


Access is tied to the Google account that was added to the drive. If you have multiple Google accounts, make sure you're signed in with the right one.
