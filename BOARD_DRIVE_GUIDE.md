# VaB HOA — Board Google Drive Guide

## The Two Shared Drives

The HOA uses two Google shared drives, both visible under **Shared drives** in your Google Drive sidebar:

**VaB Board Documents** — your primary workspace. Contains everything the board works with: meeting materials, budgets, financials, insurance, work orders, contracts, owner issues, policies, and more. Only board members can see this.

**VaB Homeowner Documents** — content that homeowners can also see. Budgets, financials, governing documents, policies, newsletters, meeting minutes, maps, forms, and reserve studies. You rarely need to go here directly — see the shortcuts below.

---

## Getting There

1. Go to **[drive.google.com](https://drive.google.com)** and sign in with the Google account that was given board access.
2. In the left sidebar, click **Shared drives**. You should see both VaB drives listed.
3. If you don't see Shared drives in the sidebar, look for a small arrow or "More" link to expand it.

On mobile, open the Google Drive app, tap the menu (≡), and tap **Shared drives**.

> If you can't see either drive, your account hasn't been added yet — contact the IT Officer.

## The → Shortcuts

At the bottom of the Board Documents root you'll see items with a **→** arrow prefix. These are shortcuts that open directly into the corresponding folder in Homeowner Documents:

- → Homeowner Budgets
- → Homeowner Financials
- → Forms
- → Governing Documents
- → Homeowner Maps
- → Meeting Minutes (Published)
- → Newsletters
- → Policies (Canonical)
- → Projects
- → Reserve Studies

When you click a shortcut, you're browsing Homeowner Documents — anything you upload there is homeowner-visible. The shortcut itself lives in Board Documents for convenience, but the content is in the other drive.

---

## What Goes Where

**The rule:** Everything homeowners can see lives in Homeowner Documents. Board Documents holds only what's private to the board, plus the → shortcuts as a convenience window into Homeowner Documents.

**Uploading something homeowner-visible** (new bylaws, published minutes, approved budget, newsletter): use the → shortcut in Board Documents to navigate to the right folder, then upload there. The file lands in Homeowner Documents — homeowners see it immediately, no second step needed.

**Uploading something board-only** (draft minutes, owner correspondence, insurance documents, working notes): upload directly to the Board Documents folder. It never touches Homeowner Documents.

When in doubt: if a homeowner could reasonably ask to see it, use the → shortcut and put it in Homeowner Documents. If it's internal board work, it stays in Board Documents.

## Uploading and Editing

- To upload a file: navigate to the right folder, then drag and drop, or use the **+ New** button.
- Google Docs, Sheets, and Slides all work normally — changes save automatically.
- Word, Excel, and PDF files can be uploaded and opened for viewing. Google Drive can also convert them to Google format for editing if you choose.
- To create a new document in the drive: navigate to the folder, click **+ New**, choose the file type. The file is stored in the drive from the start, not in your personal Drive.

---

## Searching

The search bar at the top of Drive searches across everything you have access to, including shared drives. To limit a search to just Board Documents or Homeowner Documents, navigate into that drive first, then search.

---

## A Few Things to Know

**Files belong to the organization.** Files in shared drives are owned by the HOA, not by you personally. If you leave the board, the files stay. This also means you won't see these files under "My Drive" — they live only under Shared drives.

**Don't share individual files directly with homeowners.** If a homeowner needs something, the right place for it is Homeowner Documents. Sharing individual Board Documents files directly bypasses that separation.

**Cross-drive drag and drop doesn't work.** You can't drag a file from Board Documents into Homeowner Documents (or vice versa) in the Drive UI. If you need a file in both places, use File → Make a copy and upload the copy to the other drive.

**Deleted files go to the drive's Trash**, not your personal trash. Only drive members can restore them. If you accidentally delete something, let the IT Officer know quickly — items stay in Trash for 30 days before permanent deletion.

---

## Access and Permissions

Board members are added as **Content managers**, which means you can upload, edit, move, and delete files but cannot change drive membership or settings. If you need to add someone or change permissions, contact the IT Officer.

Access is tied to the Google account that was added to the drive. If you have multiple Google accounts, make sure you're signed in with the right one.
